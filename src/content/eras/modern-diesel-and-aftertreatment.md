## Historical Context

Modern diesel engines used turbocharging, direct injection, and high-pressure fuel systems to improve torque, fuel economy, and combustion control. Their emissions profile also required careful treatment of NOx and particulates.

## Engineering Changes

Common-rail injection separated pressure generation from injection timing. Diesel particulate filters trapped soot. Selective catalytic reduction and lean NOx traps addressed nitrogen oxides. Engine control software coordinated these systems across operating conditions.

## What Remained Unresolved

The Dieselgate case showed that software, laboratory certification, real-world operation, and regulatory oversight had become inseparable. Diesel technology could no longer be understood only through mechanical hardware.
