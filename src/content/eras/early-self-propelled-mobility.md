## Historical Context

Before the automobile became a consumer product, engineers experimented with several ways to move people and goods without animal power. Steam road vehicles demonstrated that self-propelled mobility was possible, while early electric machines and batteries showed another path for urban transport.

The constraints were practical rather than merely conceptual. Roads were uneven, boilers were heavy, batteries stored little energy, and precision manufacturing was limited. Early work on pistons, cranks, connecting rods, steering, braking, and wheels created the mechanical vocabulary that later automobiles would inherit.

## What Changed

This era established the central vehicle problem: convert stored energy into controlled motion through a machine that can be started, steered, slowed, and maintained. No single propulsion system had solved the full problem yet.

## What Remained Unresolved

Fuel distribution, reliable ignition, lightweight structures, braking, and manufacturing repeatability still needed to mature before self-propelled vehicles could become widely practical.
