## Historical Context

Plug-in hybrids bridged combustion and electric-drive eras by adding external charging and larger batteries to hybrid vehicles. Their real-world energy use depended heavily on driving distance, charging access, and control strategy.

## Engineering Changes

The vehicle needed an onboard charger, larger battery, high-voltage safety systems, and operating modes that could balance electric range with engine availability. Charge-depleting and charge-sustaining operation became central concepts.

## What Remained Unresolved

Plug-in hybrids could reduce fuel use when charged regularly, but their complexity included both combustion and electric-drive systems. They also raised questions about how official test cycles should represent real charging behavior.
