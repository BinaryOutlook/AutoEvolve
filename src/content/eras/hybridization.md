## Historical Context

Hybrid vehicles used electric-drive components to reduce some limitations of combustion-only vehicles. They could recover braking energy, shut the engine off at idle, and operate the combustion engine closer to efficient regions under some conditions.

## Engineering Changes

Hybrid design made software coordination central. The vehicle had to decide when to use the engine, the motor, both, or neither. Battery state of charge, driver demand, temperature, emissions-control needs, and braking requests all affected those decisions.

## What Remained Unresolved

Hybrids reduced fuel use without requiring external charging, but they still depended on liquid fuel. Their battery capacity also limited electric-only operation compared with plug-in hybrids and battery electric vehicles.
