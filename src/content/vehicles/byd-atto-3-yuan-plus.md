## Why It Is Included

The BYD Atto 3, also sold as the Yuan Plus in some markets, is included because it turns the archive toward a global EV transition centered on China as well as North America, Europe, Japan, and Korea. It is not here as a crossover recommendation. It is here because one vehicle can introduce readers to BYD's battery origins, LFP chemistry strategy, e-Platform 3.0, and the industrial scale behind Chinese EV exports.

## Technologies Illustrated

The Atto 3 / Yuan Plus illustrates how battery chemistry and vehicle architecture are linked. BYD describes the Atto 3 as using e-Platform 3.0 and the Blade Battery, while its platform material emphasizes integration of the battery, electric drive, thermal systems, and electronic architecture. The broader lesson is that a battery is not only an energy container. It shapes floor height, crash structure, cooling, charging behavior, manufacturing flow, and cost targets.

The page also helps readers study lithium iron phosphate, or LFP, in a vehicle context. LFP generally trades some energy-density advantage for different cost, material, durability, and thermal-behavior priorities compared with nickel-rich lithium-ion chemistries. IEA analysis describes LFP's rising global share and its particular concentration in Chinese EV production. That chemistry story is one reason BYD belongs in an educational archive rather than only in a sales chart.

## Historical and Industrial Transition

This vehicle marks a shift from EV history told mainly through early adopters and low-volume experiments toward high-volume, internationally distributed electric platforms. IEA's 2025 outlook describes China as responsible for more than 70 percent of global electric car production in 2024, with supply-chain integration and manufacturing efficiency contributing to battery cost reductions. The Atto 3 / Yuan Plus is a compact way to make that industrial transition concrete.

Its inclusion also broadens the archive's geography. Modern EV history is not a single-country path. Battery supply chains, charging standards, export markets, regional regulations, and local manufacturing policy all affect which vehicles become visible and why.
