## Why Included

The Citroën DS is included because it makes the car visible as an integrated technical system. Citroën Origins notes that the DS stood out at the 1955 motor show with aerodynamic lines and hydropneumatic suspension on the front and rear wheels. Citroën comfort-history material connects the DS suspension to an engine-driven high-pressure hydraulic pump.

For this archive, the DS is not a design ranking. It is a study in system coupling: suspension, ride height, hydraulic assistance, body shape, braking, steering, and powertrain packaging were presented as parts of one engineering language.

## Technologies Illustrated

The DS illustrates hydropneumatic suspension, where hydraulic fluid and gas-filled spring elements replace a conventional steel spring-and-damper package. The important lesson is not simply softness or comfort. The suspension changed load leveling, ride height, service procedures, fluid maintenance, and the failure modes technicians had to understand.

It also illustrates hydraulic centralization. When one high-pressure system supports multiple vehicle functions, the vehicle can gain new capabilities but also becomes dependent on fluid condition, seals, pumps, accumulators, and safe pressure management.

## Historical Role

The DS extended Citroën's prewar interest in structural and packaging departures into postwar systems engineering. Where the Traction Avant emphasized front-wheel drive and unitary construction, the DS added a more ambitious hydraulic layer and a body shaped with aerodynamic intent.

Its historical role is therefore broader than one component. It shows a manufacturer treating ride, braking assistance, steering assistance, body form, and passenger comfort as a coordinated engineering problem rather than separate accessories.
