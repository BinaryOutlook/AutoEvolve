## Why Included

The Mercedes-Benz S-Class W116 is included because it shows premium-segment engineering being used as a proving ground for safety and control systems. Mercedes-Benz Public Archive material describes the W116 as the first series officially called S-Class, introduced in September 1972, with extensive passive-safety measures including a protected fuel tank location, padded interior surfaces, and controlled deformation zones.

The same archive record also identifies two later developments central to this page: the 300 SD diesel variant for the USA and Canada, and the anti-lock braking system offered from autumn 1978 in cooperation with Bosch.

## Technologies Illustrated

The W116 illustrates vehicle safety as an engineered structure rather than a single device. A reinforced passenger cell, deformation zones, protected fuel tank placement, lighting visibility, and interior impact protection all belong to the system boundary.

It also illustrates the early public arrival of electronic ABS in a production premium sedan. ABS depends on wheel-speed sensing, hydraulic modulation, and control logic. That makes it a useful bridge between mechanical braking systems and later electronic chassis-control systems.

## Historical Role

The 300 SD adds a different historical layer. Mercedes-Benz states that this S-Class diesel was developed for the USA and Canada with fleet fuel consumption in mind. That makes the W116 a case study in how regulation and fuel-consumption policy can influence even premium-car powertrain strategy.

The broader lesson is that technologies often enter through expensive or specialized vehicles before moving outward. ABS, advanced occupant protection, and electronic chassis controls did not become universal all at once; premium sedans were one visible route by which such systems moved from engineering trials to wider expectation.
