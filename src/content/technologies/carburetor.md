## Definition

A carburetor meters fuel into the intake air stream using pressure differences, jets, and mechanical linkages. It predates electronic fuel injection and was central to gasoline vehicles for much of the twentieth century.

## Why It Emerged

Early spark-ignition engines needed a practical way to vaporize and meter liquid fuel. Carburetors provided a mechanical solution that could be manufactured, adjusted, and repaired with the tools of the period.

## Why It Declined

Carburetors were limited in precision across changing temperature, altitude, engine load, and emissions requirements. Electronic fuel injection and oxygen-sensor feedback made fuel control more adaptable.
