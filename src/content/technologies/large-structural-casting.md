## Definition

Large structural casting is a manufacturing approach that forms a major vehicle
structure section as a cast metal part rather than assembling the same region
from many stamped, welded, riveted, bonded, or fastened pieces. In current EV
discussion it is often associated with aluminum body structures, but the
underlying historical theme is broader: manufacturers keep looking for ways to
reduce mass, part count, joining work, and assembly variation.

## Process Role

A simplified body-structure comparison is:

```text
many stamped parts -> fixtures -> joining -> dimensional checks
large casting -> machining or finishing -> joining to body
```

The appeal is fewer parts and potentially fewer assembly operations. The
constraint is that casting quality, alloy behavior, crash performance, heat
treatment, repairability, tooling cost, and dimensional consistency all become
more concentrated in one large part.

## Why It Matters

Lightweighting has been part of vehicle history for decades. DOE materials
sources discuss advanced high-strength steel, aluminum, magnesium, joining,
corrosion, repair, recycling, and manufacturing barriers. Large structural
casting belongs in that tradition because it changes how a vehicle body is made,
not just what material it uses.

## Tradeoffs

Large castings can simplify some assembly steps, but they can also raise tooling
investment and repair questions. A stamped multi-part structure may allow
localized replacement or design variation. A large casting may reduce interfaces
but make defects, crash damage, or late design changes more consequential.
