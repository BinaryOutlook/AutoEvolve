## Definition

A diesel engine is a compression-ignition engine. It compresses air until temperature rises enough that injected fuel can ignite without a spark plug.

## Engineering Characteristics

Diesel engines often operate with lean combustion, meaning more air is present than the chemically ideal fuel-air ratio. This can support efficiency, but it also changes emissions-control requirements, especially for NOx and particulate matter.

## Historical Role

Diesel engines became important in commercial vehicles because of durability and fuel economy. Passenger-car diesel applications expanded in some markets after high-pressure injection, turbocharging, and aftertreatment improved drivability and emissions control.
