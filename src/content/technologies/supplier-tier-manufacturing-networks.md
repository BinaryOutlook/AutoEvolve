## Definition

Supplier-tier manufacturing networks are the layered relationships among
automakers, Tier 1 suppliers, sub-tier suppliers, materials producers, tooling
companies, logistics providers, software vendors, and service organizations.
They define how a vehicle becomes a repeatable product rather than a prototype.

The common shorthand is:

```text
OEM -> Tier 1 -> Tier 2 -> materials and process suppliers
```

Real networks are messier. A supplier can be Tier 1 for one component and
sub-tier for another, and software or battery supply can cross ordinary
component boundaries.

## Why They Matter

Modern vehicles contain thousands of parts and many safety, emissions,
cybersecurity, and quality obligations. Automakers rarely manufacture every
component themselves. Instead, they specify requirements, approve designs,
validate parts, audit processes, manage logistics, and integrate supplier output
into assembly plants.

IATF 16949 matters historically because it reflects the attempt to harmonize
quality-management expectations across a global automotive supply chain.

## EV And Software Pressure

Electrification changes supplier boundaries. Battery cells, cathode materials,
power electronics, software platforms, charging interfaces, and thermal systems
can become strategic dependencies. Software-defined architecture also changes
the line between a supplied component and an updateable system function.
