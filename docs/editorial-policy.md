# Editorial Policy

AutoEvolve documents automotive technology history through neutral, source-backed explanation.

## Principles

- Explain systems before discussing vehicle examples.
- Attribute specific historical, legal, regulatory, and numerical claims.
- Avoid rankings, buying advice, and subjective review language.
- Treat controversies as documented technical and regulatory case studies.
- Mark fast-moving topics with a clear last-reviewed date.
- Use visuals to clarify systems, chronology, evidence flow, or metadata relationships rather than to decorate pages.

## Vehicle Examples

Vehicle pages must answer what broader technology the vehicle illustrates. They should not include star ratings, purchase advice, used-market pricing, or subjective driving impressions.

## Era Pages

Era pages should help readers understand chronology without flattening every region or manufacturer into the same path. Include historical context, representative cars, prominent manufacturer design choices, engineering changes, and unresolved constraints. Vehicle examples should illustrate broader technical shifts rather than serve as recommendations.

## Visuals and Figures

Every page should include at least one meaningful visual artifact where practical. Prefer sourced reference images that clarify real systems, vehicles, chronology, or evidence context. Captions should identify source, reuse basis, and limits; images should not imply claims that the surrounding cited sources cannot support.

Externally sourced images are optional. When used, include visible attribution, creator or rights holder, source link, license or reuse basis, caption, and alt text. Avoid stock-like filler, brand-advocacy imagery, misleading technical simplifications, or vehicle glamour shots that make an example page feel like a review.
